BCD Counter, 0 to 9 555 clock, decade counter, seven-segment display

A 555 timer ticks about once a second. A 74LS90 counts the ticks in BCD, a CD4511 decodes the count, and a seven-segment display shows 0 through 9 on repeat. Every part comes out of the course kit, on the kit's one breadboard.

What each chip is actually doing

U1 NE555 — the clock

Wired astable, the 555 charges C1 through RA + RB until the capacitor reaches 2/3 of the supply, then discharges it through RB alone until it falls to 1/3. Pin 3 is high while C1 charges and low while it discharges. TRIG and THRES both watch the capacitor, which is why pins 2 and 6 are tied.

Why seven resistors. The kit holds exactly one capacitor, 10 µF. A 1 s period then needs RA + 2RB near 144 kΩ, and the biggest kit resistor is 10 kΩ. Six in series make RB = 60 kΩ; RV1 adds up to 10 kΩ of trim on top.

U2 74LS90 — the count

The 74LS90 is two counters in one package: a divide-by-2 (CKA in, QA out) and a divide-by-5 (CKB in, QB QC QD out). Both advance on the falling edge of their clock. Clock the ÷2 first and feed QA into CKB, and the four outputs count in 8421 BCD:

00 0 0 050 1 0 1
10 0 0 160 1 1 0

Wire it the other way round (clock into CKB, QD into CKA) and the chip still counts to ten, but in the bi-quinary 5421 code, which the CD4511 decodes into the wrong digits.

Its four reset inputs come in pairs: R0(1) and R0(2) both high clear the count to 0, R9(1) and R9(2) both high force it to 9. All four are grounded so neither can fire. A floating 74LS input reads as high, so leaving them open freezes the display.

The trap between U2 and U3

U2 is 74LS (TTL). Its outputs only guarantee 2.7 V when high. U3 is CMOS and needs 3.5 V to be sure it is reading a 1. Wired straight across, the counter counts correctly and the display still shows wrong or blank digits.

The four 4.7 kΩ pull-ups on Q0–Q3 fix it: a 74LS output sources so little current when high that the pull-up wins and lifts the line to nearly 5 V. When the output goes low, the pull-up costs it only 1.06 mA of its 8 mA sink rating.

U3 CD4511 — the decoder

Four BCD inputs in, seven segment drives out, with its own latch, blanking and lamp test. All three are strapped inactive: /LT and /BL high, LE low. Its outputs source current, which is why the display has to be common cathode.

It draws 6 and 9 without their tails, and it blanks the display for codes 10 to 15. The 74LS90 never produces those, so a blank digit in the sequence always means a counter or pull-up fault, never a decoder fault.

The trap in the capacitor

At the top of each charge C1 is being fed only (5 − 3.33) / 70 kΩ = 24 µA. An aluminum electrolytic leaks a few microamps, more when warm or after sitting unused. 8 µA of leakage stretches the period by about half; leakage near 24 µA stops the clock completely with the display frozen on one digit.

If the clock will not start, suspect C1 before the 555. Leaving it powered for a few minutes often reforms it. Failing that, shorten the chain by one or two resistors and accept a faster count.

If something is wrong

Clock LED never lights, or stays solid

Check U1 pin 4 is at +5 V and pins 2 and 6 are joined. Then measure the timing chain unpowered: + rail to column 20 should read 70 to 80 kΩ. If all of that is right, suspect C1's leakage before the 555 (see the capacitor trap above).

Clock runs, display frozen on one digit

The counter is not seeing the clock or is held in reset. Check the jumper into U2 pin 14, and that U2 pins 2, 3, 6 and 7 are all grounded. A floating reset input reads high.

Display counts 0 to 4 and repeats, or skips

The QA to CKB jumper (U2 pin 12 to pin 1) is open.

Digits out of order

The clock went into CKB (pin 1) instead of CKA (pin 14).

Some digits blank, or wrong digits on a correct count

A missing or wrong-value pull-up on Q0–Q3, or a Q wire on the wrong CD4511 pin. Remember A is pin 7 and D is pin 6.

Display completely dark

U3 pin 4 (/BL) must be at +5 V. Then run the lamp test. If the lamp test is dark too, the commons (DS1 pins 3 and 8) are not grounded, or the display is common anode.

A segment lights in the wrong place

The display's pinout differs from the common map. Swap the two affected wires in step 24.

Everything is dead

Both module jumpers on 5 V, the module's pins lined up + to + on both sides, and a live 9 V battery.
